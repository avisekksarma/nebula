How this works

Three copies of the same store, one agreed history

Back to the lab

Quasar is a tiny replicated key-value store. A, B, and C each keep a log (the ordered history) and a map (what GET reads). The map is not copied around by itself — it is rebuilt from log entries that a majority has accepted.

Why a log

Putting x=10 into every dict works while everyone is up. It does not agree on order, or which writes survive a crash.

So a write is: append to the leader’s log, copy that entry, then — only after two of three nodes have it — mark it committed and apply it to the map.

Who leads

Everyone starts as a follower. The leader heartbeats every 250ms. If a follower hears nothing for about a second, it becomes a candidate: new term, vote for itself, ask the others.

One vote per term. Two votes win. A higher term makes a stale leader step down. PUT and DELETE go only to the current leader; a follower answers 409.

A write, in order

  1. Leader appends PUT/DELETE to its log.
  2. It copies the entry to the others.
  3. Majority (2 of 3) → commit_index moves.
  4. Each node applies committed entries, in index order, to its map.
  5. GET reads the map, never the uncommitted tail of the log.

Majority of two

Leader + one live follower can commit. One dead node does not stall the cluster. Two dead nodes cannot form a majority — the entry sits on the log and the map does not change.

A node that was down

Restart wipes RAM (empty log and map). If that log is still a prefix of the leader’s, the leader sends the missing suffix. Catch-up is “here are the operations you missed,” not “here is an opaque dump of the dict.”

The lab is this cluster with the hood open — same processes as curl to ports 8001–8003. Open the site and try a write, pause a follower, then kill the leader.
